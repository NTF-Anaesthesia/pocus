LV function

Method
Sex (sets the reference ranges)
Formulas and limits
Fractional shortening
FS = (LVIDd − LVIDs) / LVIDd × 100. PLAX M-mode just beyond the leaflet tips, perpendicular to the long axis. ≥30% normal, <20% severely impaired (older teaching values).
Teichholz
Volume = 7d³ / (2.4 + d), d in cm. EF = (EDV − ESV) / EDV. Assumes a symmetric, spheroidal LV; ASE/EACVI 2015 no longer recommends EF from linear dimensions.
Quinones
EF = S + (100 − S) × K, where S is the short-axis change (%ΔD² from diameters, or FAC from areas) and K is the apical term: normal 15%, hypokinetic 5%, akinetic 0, dyskinetic −5%, aneurysmal −10% (Quinones 1981). Using FAC for S is an estimate.
Fractional area change
FAC = (EDA − ESA) / EDA × 100, PSAX at the papillary level. No normal LV FAC cut-off is quoted: none could be verified. Do not borrow the RV FAC reference (>35%).
Volumes (Simpson's biplane)
EF = (EDV − ESV) / EDV × 100; SV = EDV − ESV. Biplane discs is the recommended 2D method. A foreshortened apex underestimates volumes.
EF categories (Lang 2015)
Normal 52–72% (male), 54–74% (female); mildly abnormal 41–51% (male), 41–53% (female); moderately abnormal 30–40%; severely abnormal <30%. LVIDd normal 4.2–5.8 cm (male), 3.8–5.2 cm (female).

Flow

Index to body surface area
Passive leg raise or fluid challenge
Formulas and limits
LVOT area
CSA = π × (d / 2)² ≈ 0.785 × d². Diameter in PLAX, mid-systole, inner edge to inner edge, at the level of the PW sample. The diameter is squared: a 1 mm error in a 20 mm LVOT changes SV by about 10%.
Stroke volume and output
SV = CSA × VTI. CO = SV × HR / 1000. Typical adult SV 60–100 mL; CI 2.5–4.0 L/min/m².
VTI
PW Doppler in the LVOT from A5C, about 0.5 cm below the valve, aligned with flow. Commonly quoted normal about 18–22 cm. VTI <16 cm marked low stroke volume in Vignon 2017.
Passive leg raise
Change = (VTI after − VTI before) / VTI before × 100. A rise ≥10–15% suggests fluid responsiveness; the Flow and fluids lecture uses ≥12%. Use the same diameter both times, so the change in VTI is the change in SV.
Body surface area
Mosteller: √(height cm × weight kg / 3600).

IVC

Breathing
Formulas and limits
Collapsibility index
(max − min) / max × 100. Spontaneous breathing: the IVC narrows in inspiration. About 40% or more: probably fluid-responsive, but a weak predictor.
Estimated RA pressure (ASE)
≤2.1 cm and collapses >50% with a sniff: 3 mmHg (0–5). >2.1 cm and collapses <50%: 15 mmHg (10–20). Anything else: 8 mmHg (5–10). Not valid on positive-pressure ventilation.
Distensibility index (Barbier)
(max − min) / min × 100. Threshold >18%.
Variability (Feissel)
(max − min) / mean × 100. Threshold >12%.
When the ventilated indices apply
Both studies used intubated patients with no respiratory effort and tidal volume >8 mL/kg predicted body weight. Raised abdominal pressure, RV failure, arrhythmia and spontaneous effort make them unreliable. Measure 1–2 cm from the RA junction, in the long axis, without the probe pressing.
